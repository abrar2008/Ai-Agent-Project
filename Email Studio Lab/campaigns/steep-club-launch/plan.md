# The Steep Club Launch: Campaign Plan

**Business:** Kettlebird Tea (sample business)
**Product:** The Steep Club, a monthly tea subscription box
**Channel:** Email only. No paid ads.
**Dates:** Monday, December 7, 2026 to Sunday, January 3, 2027 (4 weeks)
**Prep window:** Monday, November 23 to Friday, December 4, 2026

> **Before you use this plan, read this.** `brand/voice.md` is written for Prime Patchy, a patch and apparel business, not Kettlebird Tea. Its product words, taglines and sign-offs don't fit a tea shop. The general rules still apply here: plain English, no pushy sales language, and no claims we can't check (especially health claims). Kettlebird needs its own voice guide before the emails are written.

> **Facts we still need.** We don't know the box price, what's in each box, shipping costs or areas, the cancellation policy, or whether gift subscriptions exist. The plan uses `[PRICE]`, `[BOX CONTENTS]` and similar placeholders. No email should go out until these are filled in and confirmed.

---

## 1. Campaign overview

**Campaign name:** Doors Open at The Steep Club

**Summary:** Over four weeks, a short email series invites our 2,000 waitlist members to join The Steep Club, with a different angle for beginners, gift buyers and daily tea drinkers.

**Main goal:** 300 waitlist members start a paid subscription by Sunday, January 3, 2027.

That's 15% of the list. That's a high target for email alone, but possible, because these people asked to hear from us. See Risks (section 9) for what to do if we fall behind.

**Other goals**
- Learn which group joins most easily, to shape future emails.
- Collect a short "why did you join?" answer from new members, to use later as real quotes (with permission).
- Keep unsubscribes low, so the list stays healthy for next year.

---

## 2. Target audience

About 2,000 people who joined the waitlist. They already know about The Steep Club and asked to hear when it opens, so they're close to buying. The job is to answer their last questions and give them a reason to join now.

| Group | Who they are | What they want | What holds them back |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tea-curious beginners** | Like tea but don't know much about it. Mostly drink tea bags. | An easy, low-pressure way to try new teas without guessing what to buy. | "Will I like it?" "Is it too fancy for me?" "Do I need special kit?" |
| **Gift buyers** | Shopping for someone else. The window covers the holidays. | A thoughtful gift that feels personal and lasts beyond one day. | "Will it arrive in time?" "Can I choose how many months?" "Can I add a note?" |
| **Daily tea drinkers** | Drink tea every day and know what they like. | Better tea than the supermarket, new finds, and good value. | "Is it worth the price?" "Will I get teas I already have?" "Can I pause or cancel?" |

**How we know who is in which group:** Check if the waitlist form asked. If not, send a one-click "Which sounds most like you?" question in the first email (see Email 1), and treat anyone who doesn't answer as a beginner.

**Buying stage:** All three groups are at the decision stage. They need clear facts, reassurance and a reason to act, not general awareness content.

## 3. Key messages

**Core message:** Great tea, picked for you and delivered every month, so all you have to do is put the kettle on.

| Message | Best for | Proof we can use |
|---|---|---|
| **Try something new, with no guesswork.** Each box comes with simple brewing notes, so you know exactly what to do. | Beginners | Show a real brewing card. List `[BOX CONTENTS]`. |
| **A gift that keeps arriving.** Something to look forward to every month, not just on the day. | Gift buyers | Gift options and terms *only if confirmed*: `[GIFT LENGTHS]`, `[GIFT NOTE OPTION]`. |
| **Better tea for every day.** Fresh picks you won't find at the supermarket. | Daily drinkers | First box contents, the price per cup (`[PRICE]` ÷ `[CUPS PER BOX]`) if we can check it, and the teas' origins if confirmed. |
| **Easy to start, easy to stop.** Skip, pause or cancel when you like. | Everyone | Must match the real policy word for word: `[CANCELLATION POLICY]`. |

**Waitlist thank-you:** Every email should make clear these readers are first in line, because they asked to be.

**Founding member offer (needs a decision):** A small first-month offer just for the waitlist, for example `[OFFER]`, ending January 3, would give people a real reason to join now. Only use it if the owner approves it. If there's no offer, the reason to act is "first boxes go to the waitlist first."

### Claims we must not make
- **No health claims.** Don't say "detox," "boosts immunity," "calming," "helps you sleep," "aids digestion," "burns fat," "full of antioxidants" or anything like it. Talk about taste, smell, the ritual and the moment instead.
- **No delivery promises.** Never say "arrives before Christmas" or give a date unless shipping has confirmed it. Say: "We'll confirm your delivery window when you order."
- **No made-up details.** No "organic," "fair trade," "single estate" or "hand-picked" unless confirmed for that tea.
- **No fake urgency.** Only say "limited" or "last chance" if it's true.
- **No "best tea in the world," "cheapest" or similar boasts.**

## 4. Channel strategy

| Channel | Why it fits | Format | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Email (main channel)** | The whole audience is on our list and opted in. It costs almost nothing and goes straight to people who are ready to buy. | 7 planned emails per person, with some versions by group, plus 2 automatic emails | Medium |
| **Signup page on our website** | Every email links here. It must answer all the common questions in one place. | One page: what's in the box, price, how it works, gift option, FAQ, join button | Medium |
| **Organic social (optional)** | Free and helps the launch feel real. Not needed to hit the goal. | Box photos and unboxing posts, reusing email content | Low |

**Automatic emails (set up once):**
- **Welcome email:** sent right after someone subscribes. It also removes them from the launch series, so members don't get "join now" emails.
- **Unfinished checkout reminder:** one reminder, 24 hours later, only if our email tool supports it.

**Email rules for this series**
- **Pause the series** for anyone who subscribes.
- **No more than 2 emails a week** per person.
- **Avoid Christmas Eve, Christmas Day and New Year's Day** for sends.
- Follow the studio rule: drafting only. The owner sets up and sends emails in their own tool. We never send, schedule or test-send to real addresses from here.

## 7. Success metrics

**Main goal: 300 new subscribers by January 3.**

| Week | Weekly target | Running total | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Week 1 (Dec 7–13) | 120 | 120 | Launch week is usually the biggest |
| Week 2 (Dec 14–20) | 75 | 195 | Box reveal and FAQ convert the undecided |
| Week 3 (Dec 21–27) | 45 | 240 | Holiday week. Gift buyers carry it. |
| Week 4 (Dec 28–Jan 3) | 60 | 300 | New-year push and last call |

These are planning guesses, not benchmarks. We have no past results to compare against. Adjust them after Week 1.

**Other things to track**

| Measure | Target | How we track it |
|---|---|---|
| Open rate per email | 40% or higher (warm, opted-in list) | Email tool report |
| Click rate per email | 5% or higher | Email tool report |
| Signup page to checkout completed | 20% or higher | Shop or website analytics |
| Unsubscribe rate per email | Under 0.5% | Email tool report |
| Spam complaints per email | Under 0.1% | Email tool report |
| Subscribers by group | Track only (to learn which group converts) | Group tag on each subscriber |

**Reporting:** A short check every Monday: subscribers this week, running total, best and worst email, and one change to make. A full results write-up goes in `campaigns/steep-club-launch/results.md` the week of January 4.

**Checkpoint rule:** If we're below 100 subscribers by Monday, December 14, look at the click rate. If clicks are low, the email is the problem. If clicks are fine but sign-ups are low, the signup page or the price is the problem.

## 9. Risks and what to do about them

| Risk | What could happen | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **The 15% target is too high** | We end up nearer 150–200 | Track weekly. Use the spare slot for an extra reminder. Consider a founding member offer, if approved. Sending more emails won't fix a weak offer. |
| **Holiday inbox crowding** | Emails get lost among sales emails, and opens drop in Weeks 3–4 | Keep subject lines plain and personal. Send gift emails early in the week. Skip Christmas Eve and Day. |
| **Gift buyers expect Christmas delivery** | Angry customers, refunds, bad reviews | Never promise a delivery date. Say clearly in the gift emails and signup page: "We'll confirm your delivery window when you order." A printable gift note lets people give the gift on the day, even if the box comes later. |
| **The waitlist has gone cold** | People forgot they signed up, so spam complaints rise | Start Email 1 with "You joined our waitlist for The Steep Club, and it's open." Make unsubscribing easy. Watch complaints after Email 1. |
| **A health claim slips in** | Legal and trust risk | Check every email against the "must not" list in Section 3 before it goes into the email tool. |
| **Facts aren't ready by Nov 25** | The whole plan slips | Make the fact sheet the first task. If it's late, move the launch by a week instead of sending emails with guesses. |
| **Wrong voice** | Emails sound like a patch maker, not a tea shop | Write the Kettlebird voice guide before drafting (task 1). |
